01 / THE INVESTIGATOR'S MINDSET

TEST THE CLAIM.
TRACE THE TRUTH.

A feature working once is not proof that it works correctly. Testing means examining the behavior, the conditions, and the risks surrounding it.

Don't trust the screen.
Question the behavior.

My approach starts with understanding what a feature is supposed to do. From there, I design scenarios that cover normal use, invalid inputs, boundary conditions, and situations that are easy to miss.

When behavior looks wrong, the goal is not just to say "something is broken." It is to establish the conditions, reproduce the issue, compare actual and expected results, and deliver a report that supports investigation and resolution.

02 / INVESTIGATION METHOD

EVERY DEFECT
HAS A TRAIL.

These are the questions that guide a practical manual-testing investigation from the first observation to the final report.

01

What should happen?

Review the requirement, acceptance criteria, and intended user behavior before judging a result.

02

When does it fail?

Vary inputs, conditions, navigation paths, and relevant boundary values to isolate the problem.

03

Can it be reproduced?

Record the setup and exact steps so the unexpected behavior can be investigated consistently.

04

What evidence matters?

Compare expected and actual results, describe the impact, and assign a proposed priority for triage.
